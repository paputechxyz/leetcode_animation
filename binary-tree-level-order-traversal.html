102. Binary Tree Level Order Traversal — BFS with a queue

Keep a queue of nodes to visit. At the start of each level, freeze its size: pop exactly that many nodes (that's one level) while their children queue up behind them for the next level.

      Why range(len(queue))? It's evaluated once, before the loop starts. At that moment the queue holds exactly
      the current level. Children appended during the loop don't change the count, so they wait for the next round.

      Why a deque? popleft() is O(1); list.pop(0) would be O(n).
    

    

      
TimeO(n)
        Every node is appended to the queue once and popped once, and deque.popleft() is O(1).

      
SpaceO(w)
        The queue holds at most about two levels' worth of nodes, where w is the widest level. A full tree's bottom level has about n/2 nodes, so the worst case is O(n). The output list is not counted.
