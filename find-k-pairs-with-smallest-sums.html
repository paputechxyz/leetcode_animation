373. Find K Pairs with Smallest Sums — Min-heap (heapq)

Think of all sums as a grid where every row and column is sorted. Keep one candidate per row in a min-heap. Pop the smallest, then push the next cell to its right. Stop after k pops.

Sum grid (cell [i][j] = nums1[i] + nums2[j])

not reached yet in the heap (a candidate) just popped in the answer (#order) row never seeded (i ≥ k)

      Why one candidate per row is enough: row i is sorted left to right, so its smallest pair that isn't used yet is
      always the leftmost unused cell. The overall next-smallest pair must be one of these row minimums, and the heap holds
      exactly those.

      Why the seed uses only min(len(nums1), k) rows: column 0 is sorted top to bottom too, so a row
      at index ≥ k can never get into the top k: the k rows above it each have a smaller or equal pair in column 0.

      Tuples compare by sum first, so heap[0] is always the smallest sum. i and
      j break ties and tell us which cell it is.
    

    

      
TimeO(k log r)
        Here r = min(m, k), with m = len(nums1). Seeding and heapify are O(r). The loop runs at most k times, and each pass does one pop and at most one push on a heap that never holds more than r items, so each costs O(log r).

      
SpaceO(r)
        The heap keeps at most one entry per seeded row, so at most r = min(m, k) tuples. The output list adds O(k) on top of that if you count it.
