876. Middle of the Linked List — Fast & Slow Pointers

🐢 slow moves 1 node per round and 🐇 fast moves 2, so fast has always walked twice as far. When fast reaches the end, slow is exactly halfway, at the middle.

Why it works: after k rounds slow is at index k and fast is at index 2k. The loop stops when fast can't take two more steps, i.e. 2k ≈ n − 1 (or n). So k ≈ n / 2, which is the middle.

      
Odd length (n = 5): fast stops on the last node (fast.next is None). Slow is at index 2, the exact middle.

      Even length (n = 6): fast steps off the end onto None. Slow is at index 3, the second of the two middles, which is what LeetCode wants.

      
Why check fast.next? fast.next.next would crash with None.next if fast.next were None. The fast check protects fast.next in the same way.

    

    

      
TimeO(n)
        n = number of nodes. Fast visits about n nodes two at a time, so the loop runs ⌊n/2⌋ rounds of O(1) work. It's one pass, without counting the length first.

      
SpaceO(1)
        Just two pointer variables. No array copy of the list and no recursion.
